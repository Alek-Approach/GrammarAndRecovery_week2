Grammar Focus A: Simple or continuous?

Present simple

I/you/we/they work · he/she/it works

Facts and things that are always trueWater freezes at 0°C.
Habits and routinesI check my email every morning.
Permanent situationsShe teaches at a university.
Timetables and schedulesThe train leaves at 7:15.
Present continuous

am / is / are + -ing

Actions in progress nowSorry, I'm talking to a client.
Temporary situationsI'm staying with my sister this month.
Changing or developing situationsPrices are rising.
Annoying habits (with always)He's always interrupting me!

Stative verbs describe states, not actions, and are not normally used in the continuous: know, believe, understand, mean, remember, want, need, prefer, like, love, hate, own, belong, seem, contain, consist, depend.

Verbs with two meanings

VerbState: simpleAction: continuous
thinkI think it's true. (opinion)I'm thinking about it. (considering)
haveI have a car. (possess)I'm having lunch. (eat / experience)
seeI see what you mean. (understand)I'm seeing a client at 3. (meet)
taste / smellIt tastes sweet. (quality)He's tasting the soup. (action)
beHe is rude. (his character)He's being rude. (his behaviour now)

Grammar Focus B: Present perfect or past simple?

Present perfect: have / has + past participle

Life experience (time not stated)Have you ever worked abroad? I've never been to Peru.
Very recent action (just)I've just sent the email.
Sooner than expected (already)She's already finished the report.
Expected but not happened (yet)Have you replied yet? I haven't replied yet.
Past action, result nowI've lost my keys, so I can't get in.
Unfinished time periodI've had three meetings today.

Word position: just / already / ever / never go between have and the past participle. Yet goes at the end of questions and negatives.

Past simple

Finished time. You say or know when.

I finished my degree in 2021. When did you arrive?

Present perfect

Unfinished time, or a connection to now. No finished time.

I've finished my degree, so now I'm looking for work.

Never use the present perfect with a finished-time expression: yesterday, last week, in 2019, two days ago, when I was a child.

In everyday American English, people often use the past simple with just, already and yet (Did you eat yet?). In this course, use the present perfect in formal writing.

Grammar Focus C: Present perfect simple or continuous?

Form: have / has been + -ing

An activity that started in the past and continues now (how long, for, since)I've been studying English since 2020.
A recent activity with a visible result nowYour hands are dirty. Have you been gardening?
Present perfect simple

Focus on the result or completion. How many? How much?

I've written three emails this morning.

Present perfect continuous

Focus on the activity and its duration. How long?

I've been writing emails all morning.

  • Stative verbs use the simple form: I've known her for years. (not have been knowing)
  • With live, work, study and teach, there is little difference: I've lived / I've been living here for five years.

Grammar Focus D: Telling a story in the past

TenseFormUseExample
Past simpleworked, went, didn't goThe main events, in order.I opened the door and saw a stranger.
Past continuouswas / were + -ingAn action in progress, interrupted by another (when); two actions at the same time (while); background description.I was cooking when the phone rang. While I was cooking, he was setting the table. The sun was shining.
Past perfecthad + past participleAn action before another past action. Common with by the time, already, before, when.By the time we arrived, the film had already started.
Past perfect continuous (B2+)had been + -ingDuration of an activity up to a past moment.I had been waiting for an hour when the bus came.
  • Don't overuse the past perfect. Use it only when you need to show that something happened earlier. After often makes the order clear: After I (had) checked my email, I left.
  • Stative verbs are not used in the past continuous: I knew the answer. (not was knowing)

Error correction workshop

Activate17 minPairs in breakout rooms

These sentences come from anonymized student writing (Diagnostic Task 1: Describe an important change in your life). Two sentences are correct. For each sentence, decide if it is correct. If not, write the corrected sentence and say whether it is an error of form or use.

Form error: The verb is built incorrectly (wrong past form, wrong auxiliary, wrong participle). Example: I have went.

Use error: The verb is built correctly, but it is the wrong tense for the meaning or time. Example: I have seen her yesterday.
